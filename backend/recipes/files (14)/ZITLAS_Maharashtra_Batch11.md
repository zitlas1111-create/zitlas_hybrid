## ZITLAS-REC-0521 — ZITLAS Bhagar Upma-Style Bowl [Breakfast]
Proso millet (bhagar/vari), traditionally eaten during fasting, cooked upma-style with peanuts — a genuinely new grain for the database, distinct from any other millet already used.
Meal: Breakfast | Goals: Weight Loss | Diet: Vegan | Region: Maharashtrian-inspired
Servings: 1 | Prep: 10 min | Cook: 15 min | Total: 25 min | Difficulty: Easy | Cost: Moderate | Equipment: Kadai

**Ingredients**
- Bhagar (proso millet) — 50 g
- Peanuts — 15 g
- Potato, cubed — 30 g
- Oil — 6 ml
- Cumin seeds, green chilli — 3 g

**Instructions**
1. Rinse bhagar and soak for 10 minutes.
2. Heat oil, temper cumin seeds and green chilli, roast peanuts, add potato and saute 2-3 minutes.
3. Add the soaked bhagar and water (1:2 ratio), cover and simmer 10-12 minutes until soft and fluffy.
4. Serve hot.

**Nutrition/serving (estimated):** Calories ~335 kcal | Protein ~10.7 g | Carbs ~43.6 g | Fat ~13.9 g | Fiber ~2.9 g
**Primary protein sources:** Peanuts
**Why it works:** Bhagar is a fasting-day grain rarely represented in fitness recipe collections, with a distinct texture from rice or wheat-based millets already in the database; Traditionally paired with potato and peanuts for a complete fasting meal; Naturally gluten-free
**Tags:** High Fiber | Hostel Friendly: No | ZITLAS Original: No

## ZITLAS-REC-0580 — ZITLAS Jambhul Sherbet Drink [Drinks]
A diluted, drinkable java plum sherbet — distinct from the curd bowl or frozen sorbet uses of jambhul already in this collection by being a simple sipping drink.
Meal: Beverage | Goals: Weight Loss | Diet: Vegan | Region: Maharashtrian-inspired
Servings: 1 | Prep: 10 min | Cook: 0 min | Total: 10 min | Difficulty: Easy | Cost: Moderate | Equipment: Mixer

**Ingredients**
- Jambhul (java plum), deseeded — 100 g
- Water — 150 ml
- Black salt — 1 g

**Instructions**
1. Blend deseeded jambhul with water and black salt until smooth.
2. Strain if a smoother texture is preferred.
3. Chill and serve.

**Nutrition/serving (estimated):** Calories ~60 kcal | Protein ~0.7 g | Carbs ~15.6 g | Fat ~0.2 g | Fiber ~0.6 g
**Primary protein sources:** None (not a protein-focused recipe)
**Why it works:** Simple sipping-drink format is the third distinct use of jambhul in this collection, alongside the curd bowl and frozen sorbet; Black salt is a traditional pairing that balances the fruit's tartness; Very low calorie, seasonal

## ZITLAS-REC-0581 — ZITLAS Curd Karvand Lassi Drink [Drinks]
A lassi made by blending curd with karvand instead of the usual mango or plain sweetened version — a distinct tart-savoury lassi flavour not used anywhere else in the database.
Meal: Beverage | Goals: Weight Loss | Diet: Vegetarian | Region: Konkan-inspired
Servings: 1 | Prep: 8 min | Cook: 0 min | Total: 8 min | Difficulty: Easy | Cost: Moderate | Equipment: Mixer

**Ingredients**
- Curd — 150 g
- Karvand (Konkan berries) — 30 g
- Water — 50 ml
- Black salt — 1 g

**Instructions**
1. Blend curd, karvand, water and black salt together until smooth.
2. Chill and serve.

**Nutrition/serving (estimated):** Calories ~99 kcal | Protein ~5.4 g | Carbs ~9.2 g | Fat ~5.0 g | Fiber ~0.6 g
**Primary protein sources:** Curd
**Why it works:** Karvand as a lassi flavour is genuinely distinct from the mango-based lassi already in the database, offering a tart-savoury alternative rather than a sweet one; Curd provides real protein; Regional Konkan ingredient in a familiar drink format
