## ZITLAS-REC-0151 — ZITLAS Jowar Roti with Peanut Curry
A sorghum (jowar) flatbread served with a light peanut-based curry — a new grain base paired with a curry rather than a dry chutney.
Meal: Breakfast | Goals: General Fitness, Weight Loss | Diet: Vegan
Servings: 1 | Prep: 10 min | Cook: 12 min | Total: 22 min | Difficulty: Moderate | Cost: Budget | Equipment: Tawa, Kadai

**Ingredients**
- Jowar flour — 60 g
- Peanuts, ground — 25 g
- Tomato, chopped — 30 g
- Oil — 6 ml
- Cumin seeds, turmeric — 3 g

**Instructions**
1. Knead jowar flour with warm water (it needs warm water to bind) and pat into a flatbread; cook on a hot tawa.
2. For the curry, heat oil, temper cumin, add tomato and cook soft, then stir in ground peanuts with water to form a light curry.
3. Simmer 5 minutes.
4. Serve the roti hot with the peanut curry.

**Nutrition/serving (estimated):** Calories ~410 kcal | Protein ~13.0 g | Carbs ~48.7 g | Fat ~19.3 g | Fiber ~6.5 g
**Primary protein sources:** Peanuts
**Why it works:** Jowar is a distinct gluten-free grain from bajra or ragi already in the database; Curry format (not dry chutney) changes how the peanuts are eaten; Naturally gluten-free
**Tags:** High Fiber | Hostel Friendly: No | ZITLAS Original: No

## ZITLAS-REC-0152 — ZITLAS Foxtail Millet Pongal
A South Indian pongal made with foxtail millet and moong dal, tempered with ghee, pepper and cumin — a distinct one-pot format from any upma or khichdi.
Meal: Breakfast | Goals: General Fitness | Diet: Vegetarian | Region: South Indian-inspired
Servings: 1 | Prep: 8 min | Cook: 15 min | Total: 23 min | Difficulty: Easy | Cost: Budget | Equipment: Pressure cooker

**Ingredients**
- Foxtail millet — 50 g
- Moong dal (dry) — 25 g
- Ghee — 8 g
- Black pepper, cumin seeds, ginger — 4 g

**Instructions**
1. Dry-roast moong dal lightly, then pressure cook with foxtail millet and water (1:4 ratio) for 3 whistles until soft and porridge-like.
2. Heat ghee, temper crushed black pepper, cumin seeds and chopped ginger.
3. Pour the tempering over the cooked pongal and mix.
4. Serve hot.

**Nutrition/serving (estimated):** Calories ~334 kcal | Protein ~12.2 g | Carbs ~47.2 g | Fat ~10.4 g | Fiber ~8.0 g
**Primary protein sources:** Moong dal
**Why it works:** Pongal's soft, tempered rice-porridge technique is genuinely different from khichdi or upma preparation; Foxtail millet swap keeps the traditional method but changes the grain profile; Traditional South Indian temple food reframed as a fitness breakfast
**Tags:** One Pot | Hostel Friendly: No | ZITLAS Original: No

## ZITLAS-REC-0154 — ZITLAS Whole Green Gram Paratha
A stuffed paratha filled with whole cooked green gram (moong, not split) — a chunkier, whole-legume filling distinct from the split-dal Moong Dal Paratha.

## ZITLAS-REC-0160 — ZITLAS Air-Fried Urad Dal Vada Bowl
Urad dal vadas air-fried and served dry with chutney rather than soaked in curd — a distinct bowl format from the Air-Fried Moong Dahi Vada.
