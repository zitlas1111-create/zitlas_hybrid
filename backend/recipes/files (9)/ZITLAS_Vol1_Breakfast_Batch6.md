## ZITLAS-REC-0251 — ZITLAS Baked Aloo Paratha
A classic potato-stuffed paratha, but roasted with a bare minimum of oil rather than the usual generous ghee — a genuinely lighter version of the most common Indian breakfast paratha.
Meal: Breakfast | Goals: General Fitness | Diet: Vegan | Region: Punjabi-inspired
Servings: 1 | Prep: 12 min | Cook: 10 min | Total: 22 min | Difficulty: Moderate | Cost: Budget | Equipment: Tawa

**Ingredients**
- Potato, boiled and mashed — 100 g
- Whole wheat atta — 70 g
- Oil — 6 ml
- Green chilli, coriander, ajwain — 5 g

**Instructions**
1. Mash boiled potato with green chilli, coriander and ajwain.
2. Knead atta into a dough, stuff with the potato mixture and seal well.
3. Roll out gently and roast on a hot tawa using only a light brushing of oil rather than pooling it.
4. Serve hot with curd.

**Nutrition/serving (estimated):** Calories ~378 kcal | Protein ~10.3 g | Carbs ~69.0 g | Fat ~7.3 g | Fiber ~9.6 g
**Primary protein sources:** None (not a protein-focused recipe)
**Why it works:** Deliberately low-oil roasting technique keeps this meaningfully lighter than a typical restaurant aloo paratha; The most requested classic paratha, finally represented in the database; Filling, familiar comfort food

## ZITLAS-REC-0254 — ZITLAS Thalipeeth
A Maharashtrian multi-flour savoury pancake made from a blend of jowar, bajra and besan — an entirely different cooking technique from any chilla, dosa or paratha, mixed and patted directly on the tawa.
Meal: Breakfast | Goals: General Fitness, Weight Loss | Diet: Vegan | Region: Maharashtrian-inspired
Servings: 1 | Prep: 10 min | Cook: 10 min | Total: 20 min | Difficulty: Moderate | Cost: Budget | Equipment: Tawa

**Ingredients**
- Jowar flour — 25 g
- Bajra flour — 25 g
- Besan — 20 g
- Onion, finely chopped — 25 g
- Oil — 6 ml
- Cumin seeds, ajwain — 3 g

**Instructions**
1. Mix the three flours with onion, cumin seeds and ajwain, and enough water to form a soft, pliable dough.
2. Pat the dough directly onto a hot, lightly oiled tawa with wet hands into a thin round (a distinct technique from rolling with a pin).
3. Poke a few holes in the centre with a finger, drizzle a little oil, and cook covered until golden on both sides.
4. Serve hot with curd.

**Nutrition/serving (estimated):** Calories ~312 kcal | Protein ~10.0 g | Carbs ~48.8 g | Fat ~8.9 g | Fiber ~7.1 g
**Primary protein sources:** Besan
**Why it works:** Multi-flour blend gives a broader nutrient profile than any single-grain flatbread in the database; Hand-patted-on-the-tawa technique is genuinely different from rolling a dough with a pin; Traditional Maharashtrian breakfast staple
**Tags:** High Fiber | Hostel Friendly: No | ZITLAS Original: No

## ZITLAS-REC-0258 — ZITLAS Baked Moong Dal Kachori
A whole wheat kachori filled with spiced moong dal, baked instead of deep-fried — a distinct pastry-like format not represented anywhere else in the database.
Meal: Breakfast | Goals: Muscle Gain, General Fitness | Diet: Vegan | Region: Rajasthani-inspired
Servings: 2 | Prep: 25 min | Cook: 20 min | Total: 45 min | Difficulty: Moderate | Cost: Moderate | Equipment: Oven

**Ingredients**
- Moong dal, soaked and coarsely ground — 70 g
- Whole wheat atta — 100 g
- Oil — 10 ml
- Fennel seeds, ginger, asafoetida — 5 g

**Instructions**
1. Cook the ground moong dal with fennel seeds, ginger and asafoetida in a dry pan until thick and moisture-free; cool.

## ZITLAS-REC-0261 — ZITLAS Khaman
A fluffy, spongy steamed besan snack made with a different batter ratio and technique from dhokla, giving it a distinctly lighter, more porous texture.
Meal: Breakfast | Goals: Weight Loss | Diet: Vegetarian | Region: Gujarati-inspired
Servings: 2 | Prep: 15 min | Cook: 15 min | Total: 30 min | Difficulty: Moderate | Cost: Budget | Equipment: Pressure cooker

**Ingredients**
- Besan — 100 g
- Curd — 30 g
- Water — 80 ml
- Eno fruit salt — 4 g
- Oil — 6 ml
- Mustard seeds, green chilli — 4 g

**Instructions**
1. Whisk besan with curd and water into a thin, well-beaten batter (beat vigorously for extra air, unlike a standard dhokla batter).
2. Add eno fruit salt and mix gently just before steaming.
3. Pour into a greased tin and steam for 12-15 minutes until well risen and spongy.

**Nutrition/serving (estimated):** Calories ~431 kcal | Protein ~23.1 g | Carbs ~59.4 g | Fat ~13.0 g | Fiber ~10.8 g

## ZITLAS-REC-0263 — ZITLAS Instant Rava Dosa
A thin, lacy, crisp dosa made from a rava-rice flour batter that needs no fermentation — a distinctly different texture and technique from the fermented urad-rice dosas already in the database.
Meal: Breakfast | Goals: Weight Loss | Diet: Vegan | Region: South Indian-inspired
Servings: 1 | Prep: 15 min | Cook: 10 min | Total: 25 min | Difficulty: Moderate | Cost: Budget | Equipment: Tawa

**Ingredients**
- Rava — 40 g
- Rice flour — 20 g
- Onion, finely chopped — 20 g
- Oil — 8 ml
- Cumin seeds, green chilli — 3 g

**Instructions**
1. Whisk rava and rice flour with plenty of water into a thin, watery batter (much thinner than a regular dosa batter); rest 15 minutes.
2. Mix in onion, cumin seeds and green chilli.
3. Pour the batter from height around the edges of a hot tawa first, then fill the centre, to create lacy holes.
4. Drizzle oil around the edges and cook until crisp and golden; no flipping needed.
5. Serve hot with chutney.

**Nutrition/serving (estimated):** Calories ~296 kcal | Protein ~5.6 g | Carbs ~47.4 g | Fat ~8.7 g | Fiber ~1.7 g
**Primary protein sources:** None (not a protein-focused recipe)

## ZITLAS-REC-0264 — ZITLAS Neer Dosa
An ultra-thin, soft rice dosa from Mangalore made without fermentation or urad dal — a distinctly different rice-only batter and delicate texture from any other dosa in the database.

**Ingredients**
- Rice, soaked and ground — 60 g
- Water — 120 ml
- Oil — 5 ml
- Salt — 1 g

**Instructions**
1. Soak rice for at least 2 hours, then grind with water into a very thin, watery batter, thinner than any other dosa batter used elsewhere.

## ZITLAS-REC-0265 — ZITLAS Appam with Coconut Milk
A Kerala fermented rice-and-coconut-milk pancake, cooked in a curved pan for soft, spongy centres and lacy, crisp edges — a bowl-shaped format entirely unlike a flat dosa or uttapam.
Meal: Breakfast | Goals: General Fitness | Diet: Vegan | Region: Kerala-inspired
Servings: 2 | Prep: 30 min | Cook: 15 min | Total: 45 min | Difficulty: Moderate | Cost: Moderate | Equipment: Tawa

**Ingredients**
- Rice, soaked and ground — 100 g
- Coconut milk — 60 ml
- Water — 40 ml
- Sugar or jaggery, yeast — 4 g

**Instructions**
1. Soak rice for several hours, grind with a little of the coconut milk into a smooth batter, mix in yeast and a pinch of sugar, and let ferment overnight.
2. Just before cooking, thin the batter with the remaining coconut milk and water.
3. Heat a curved appam pan (or a small kadai), pour a ladle of batter and swirl quickly up the sides.
4. Cover and cook until the centre is soft and set and the edges are golden and lacy.
5. Serve as is or with a light vegetable stew.
6. Note: batter needs overnight fermentation, not counted in active prep time above.

**Nutrition/serving (estimated):** Calories ~504 kcal | Protein ~7.4 g | Carbs ~83.3 g | Fat ~15.8 g | Fiber ~3.7 g

**Why it works:** The curved-pan technique produces a bowl shape with a distinct soft-centre, crisp-edge texture unlike any flat dosa or uttapam in the database; Coconut milk gives a naturally mild sweetness without added sugar; Classic Kerala breakfast staple, genuinely different from Tamil or Karnataka rice preparations already represented
